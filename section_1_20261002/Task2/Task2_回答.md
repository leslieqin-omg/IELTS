# 作文正文

In recent years, the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence has sparked widespread debate regarding its impact on the job market. While some individuals worry that automation will lead to widespread unemployment, others believe it will create new opportunities and boost productivity. In my view, whether AI displaces human workers depends largely on the nature of the profession.

On the one hand, jobs that involve routine and repetitive tasks are most vulnerable to being replaced by artificial intelligence. This is because these positions do not require complex decision-making or deep intellectual engagement. Instead, employees primarily follow standardized, step-by-step procedures that can be efficiently executed by automated algorithms. For example, assembly line workers and customer service agents are increasingly being substituted by intelligent robotics and conversational chatbots. Consequently, workers in these fields face severe threats of displacement unless they undergo retraining.

On the other hand, professions that require creativity and empathy are unlikely to be completely replaced by machines. Such roles demand not only original ideas but also emotional intelligence and nuanced judgment. Take drug discovery and education as prime examples. Although molecular researchers can utilize deep-learning tools such as AlphaFold and diffusion models to design novel protein binders or nanobodies, the initial biological targets must still be identified through literature review and validated by human scientists in wet-lab experiments. Similarly, AI lacks genuine feelings and cannot put itself in students' shoes to provide tailored emotional guidance. Therefore, rather than being substituted, these professionals will use AI as a tool to enhance their overall efficiency.

In conclusion, although artificial intelligence will inevitably eliminate routine manual and clerical positions, it cannot fully replace roles that rely on creative thinking and human connection. As long as professionals adapt and leverage AI as a supportive instrument, technological progress will drive productivity rather than mass unemployment.
